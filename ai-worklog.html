NXTWAVE GROWTH CHALLENGE · ROUND 1

AI worklog

Three real iterations from this submission: what was asked, what AI produced, what I changed, and what shipped.

This challenge is a simulation. No students were contacted. The registration figures in the plan and console are assumptions or synthetic examples.
01 / Plan

From a target to measurable distribution

Prompt

“This is the docx and we need this to be built and win this for internship and give me a plan.”

AI output

A campus referral plan: 20 student connectors × 18 registrations, seven clubs × 30, and a 12% planning adjustment from 570 gross to roughly 502 unique.

What I changed or rejected

I kept the arithmetic but rejected presenting it as evidence that 500 registrations were likely or achieved. The adjustment is an explicit assumption, and success is measured by eligible, deduplicated registrations.

Final output

The five-slide growth plan shows the math, the seven-day operating model, and the assumptions beside the target.

02 / Asset

From a visual demo to an honest prototype

Prompt

“Now complete the work and we will submit it.”

AI output

A live referral console with sample totals of 328 unique and 361 gross, connector kits, a student form, and a dashboard.

What I changed or rejected

The first version could make seeded counts look like campaign results. It also counted an ineligible sample entry in the unique total and created malformed referral links when a source URL already had a code. I corrected those behaviors and made the synthetic status visible.

Final output

The live sandbox labels its sample numbers, accepts example.com demo emails, links a connector to the form, and holds the unique total steady for duplicates or ineligible years.

03 / Submission

From a generic last slide to testable judgment

Prompt

“Make this in best format.”

AI output

A polished five-slide deck whose final slide listed a 48-hour production schedule.

What I changed or rejected

After opening the actual submission form, I found a required “Your 3 Tests” field. The schedule described work already done and used space that could show how I would learn during a real campaign.

Final output

The final slide now proposes three experiments with an explicit decision metric: trusted sender, outcome-led copy, and one reminder.
